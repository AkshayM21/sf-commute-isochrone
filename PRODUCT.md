# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People comparing where to live in San Francisco based on the door-to-door transit commute to a workplace they choose. They use the map to evaluate neighborhoods, inspect realistic route choices, and understand which transit services are interchangeable without needing expert knowledge of every agency or line.

## Product Purpose

Show which parts of San Francisco keep a chosen workplace within an acceptable commute, then make the result inspectable down to the actual walk, transit, transfer, and service-risk details. Success means a user can compare neighborhoods confidently, understand the useful route choices behind a travel time, and distinguish true flexibility from superficially similar but impractical alternatives.

## Brand Personality

Analytical, calm, practical. The interface is map-first and direct. It explains uncertainty and route optionality without hype, decoration, or transit-planner jargon.

## Anti-references

Do not become a marketing landing page, a gamified real-estate score, or an overdecorated transit app. Avoid dense route diagrams that require expert interpretation, decorative motion, unexplained abbreviations, and claims that services are interchangeable when their boarding corridor or destination-facing branch differs materially.

## Design Principles

1. Keep the housing decision primary: every detail should help compare where a person could live.
2. Show the route behind the number: travel times, alternatives, and risk must remain inspectable.
3. Compress complexity without hiding it: group genuinely interchangeable services, but preserve meaningful boarding and destination-branch differences.
4. Prefer structural truth over local lore: derive route families and labels from the transit network, not hardcoded line knowledge.
5. Make uncertainty legible: distinguish schedule time, bad-day risk, and optionality without overstating precision.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Accessibility is a pragmatic guardrail rather than the primary redesign driver. Preserve keyboard access and reduced-motion behavior, maintain readable contrast, and pair route color with text or shape so important distinctions do not depend on color alone. Target WCAG AA when it is achievable without disproportionate complexity.
